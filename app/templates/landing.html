The hierarchy of disparity

Black–White economic gaps are not uniform: measured as disparity ratios they range from about 1.6× to 5.5× — a roughly three-and-a-half-fold spread depending on which dimension you measure. Wealth and imprisonment show the starkest ratios: Black Americans are imprisoned at 5.5× the White rate (2010–2020 average) and hold just 18 % of White median household wealth (2022) — a 5.5× wealth ratio in the other direction. Income and homeownership gaps, though severe, are narrower at roughly 1.6×. This chart ranks the six dimensions by their Black-to-White disparity ratio, where 1.0 would mean perfect parity. Each dimension is measured over its own window and by its own agency — the ratios are comparable in direction and magnitude, not in vintage.

The north-star question

If a single number anchors this project it is the Black–White median wealth gap. Measured by the Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances across twelve triennial waves (1989–2022), it fell to 8.2 % in the aftermath of the Great Recession (2013 survey) — the lowest reading since 1989 — before rebounding to 18.2 % by 2022, the highest on record and still less than one fifth of White median wealth. The series minimum, 6.0 % in 1989, rests on a small subsample we flag as suspect; see the wealth-gap page for that caveat.
