Share of adults aged 25 and over with a bachelor's degree or higher, by race, 2006–2022 — the Census American Community Survey (table C15002) across sixteen annual estimates.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey, table C15002 (Educational attainment by sex by age), race "alone" universe, 2006–2022 one-year estimates. Bachelor's-or-higher is the sum of bachelor's, master's, professional, and doctoral degrees divided by the population aged 25+. 2020 is missing: the Census Bureau released no standard ACS 1-year estimates for that year, so the window holds sixteen estimates, not seventeen, and the lines below run straight from 2019 to 2021 — that stretch is a gap in the data, not a measured trend. DuBois reconstructs these public-domain data; the original ACS tables remain authoritative.

Sixteen annual estimates, 2006–2022. There is no 2020 point — the Census Bureau cancelled the standard ACS 1-year release that year — so each line is drawn straight across the 2019–2021 interval.

Progress, but parity nowhere near

Every group gained ground over the window — college attainment rose across the board. But the gains were uneven. By 2022, 25.4 % of Black adults held a bachelor's degree or higher, against 39.0 % of White adults and 57.4 % of Asian adults. The Black–White gap did not close: it stood at 11.7 percentage points in 2006 and 13.6 points in 2022 — slightly wider, not narrower, after sixteen years.

Read that widening carefully. The gap is flat between 11.4 and 12.0 points for every one of the fourteen years from 2006 to 2019, then steps to 13.5 points in 2021 and stays there. The step coincides with the 2021 ACS vintage, in which White attainment jumps 3.9 points in a single interval against a long-run average of about half a point a year. The honest reading is that the gap was stable for fourteen measured years and that the widening sits entirely across the 2019–2021 break, where the survey itself changed. We report the arithmetic as published rather than smooth it, but it should not be read as a trend. See Methodology & Provenance.

Asian attainment pulled furthest ahead, climbing from 49.2 % to 57.4 %. Black attainment rose from 16.9 % to 25.4 % — real, measurable progress in absolute terms, yet the relative position barely moved: Black attainment in 2022 (25.4 %) remained below where White attainment had already been in 2006 (28.6 %).
