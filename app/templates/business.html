Business Ownership

Share of U.S. employer firms by race of owner, 2018–2021 — the Census Annual Business Survey (ABS) across four annual snapshots. In 2021 the five race groups account for 5,695,910 employer firms, or 96.64 % of the universe; a sixth published group, Hispanic ownership, is an ethnicity crosscut that overlaps them and cannot be added on top.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, Annual Business Survey (ABS), Company Summary — employer firms by race of owner, 2018–2021, classified by the reported race of the majority owner(s). The categories are not mutually exclusive and do not exhaust the universe. The file's sixth group, race_group 91 (Hispanic), is an ethnicity crosscut: a Hispanic-owned firm is also counted in whichever of the White, Black, Asian or other race rows its owners report. So the five race rows — and only those five — form the partition: in 2021 they sum to 96.64 %, the remaining ~3.4 % being employer firms the survey assigns to none of them. Adding the Hispanic row on top gives 98.33 %, but that figure double-counts and is not a coverage rate. The race-row coverage is 96.19 % (2018), 96.48 % (2019), 96.56 % (2020) and 96.64 % (2021). Where the provenance lives: this series has no row in data_dictionary.csv, which covers only part of the collection. Its record sits in series_registry.json instead, as series D1012 — "Employer Firms by Owner Race", source Census ABS Company Summary, years 2018–2021. DuBois reconstructs these public-domain data; the original ABS tables remain authoritative.

The six bars are the owner groups the ABS publishes. Only the five race bars partition the universe, and in 2021 they cover 96.64 % of employer firms; the rest are unassigned. The Hispanic bar is an ethnicity crosscut drawn from the same firms, so it must be read alongside the race bars, never added to them.

Underrepresentation at the ownership frontier

In 2021, Black-owned firms accounted for 2.73 % of U.S. employer firms — 161,031 of them. The five race groups together account for 5,695,910 firms, 96.64 % of the universe; White-owned firms alone held 82.0 %. Yet Black Americans make up about 12–13 % of the U.S. population (12.18 % in 2022, the nearest year this collection measures). If ownership matched population share — 12.18 % against 2.73 % — Black-owned firms would number roughly four and a half times as many.

The Black share did rise over the four-year window — from 2.18 % in 2018 to 2.73 % in 2021, a gain of roughly half a percentage point. Asian-owned firms held steady near 10–11 % of the total, and White-owned firms remained above 82 % every year. The ownership frontier, in other words, is dominated by one group to a degree that the population shares do not begin to explain.
