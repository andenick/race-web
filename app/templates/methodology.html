Race-category harmonization

Race terminology follows US Census conventions: the "alone" universe (not "alone or in combination"). Hispanic is treated as a separate ethnicity rather than a race category. In the ACS race-iteration suffixes that means I is the Hispanic or Latino iteration, while H is White alone, not Hispanic or Latino — the non-Hispanic White benchmark that appears in our income series as the White nH columns. The SCF uses its own RACE coding: 1989–2019 = White/Black/Hispanic/Other (no Asian); 2022 adds an Asian category — not back-imputed.

Known limit: the 1989 Black-wealth value (6.0 %) is suspect, resting on a small Black subsample and on the SCF race coding used in that wave. It is flagged v2-verify and excluded from synthesis findings.

Validation

Cross-source validation is run by the upstream construction pipeline, not by this site. Its result is recorded in the validation field of series_registry.json, which reports V10 (P10) 7/7 and V_ALL (consolidated) 16/16 passing across the Wealth, Employment, Income, Poverty, Housing, Criminal justice and Slave trade panels. No validator artifact ships in this repository, so treat that line as the pipeline's own record rather than something you can re-run here.

No values on this site are fabricated by us, and we interpolate nothing: where a year is missing it stays missing rather than being filled in. Where an original source itself publishes imputed estimates, we say so and carry the figure through as published — the trans-Atlantic slave-trade series is the case that matters, since SlaveVoyages reports embarked and disembarked counts as TAST imputed totals that correct for voyages with incomplete records. Those are the source's imputations, labelled as such on the history page, not ours.

Known gaps and caveats

Each of these is a real limitation of the underlying data. We list them because a chart alone will not show them.

  • 2020 is missing everywhere the ACS is the source. The Census Bureau did not release standard 1-year ACS estimates for 2020, so there is no 2020 row in the income, poverty, homeownership, educational-attainment or race-share series. Charts that appear to span 2005–2022 (2006–2022 for attainment) in fact have a one-year hole, and their lines simply bridge it.
  • 2025 unemployment is a partial year. The annual unemployment file records n_months = 6 for 2025, so the 2025 point is a six-month average rather than a full-year one, and is not strictly comparable with the years before it. It is plotted, but it is excluded from every multi-year average quoted on this site: the headline Black/White unemployment ratio of 2.12× is the mean over the 53 complete years 1972–2024. The only other short observation is Hispanic unemployment in 1973 (n_months = 10); no figure on this site is drawn from it.
  • The 2021 ACS vintage is a break, not just a gap. Beyond the missing 2020 row, the 2021 release itself is not cleanly comparable with 2019. White bachelor's-or-higher attainment steps up 3.9 points across that interval against a long-run average near half a point a year, and the White homeownership rate steps up 2.9 points — both far larger than any other year-on-year move in their series. Any comparison that straddles 2019–2021, including the apparent widening of the education and homeownership gaps at the end of the window, partly reflects a change in the survey rather than a change in the world. We carry the published figures through unsmoothed and flag them on the affected pages.
  • The data dictionary is incomplete. data_dictionary.csv documents 12 of the 20 data CSVs. Eight have no dictionary row — homeownership, imprisonment, business ownership, metro income, the three slave-trade files and the 2022 wealth-gap summary. Their provenance is recorded in series_registry.json instead.
  • The region-level slave-trade file is a partial subset. slavetrade_by_region.csv covers 10,367 voyages and 2,891,050 people disembarked, against 36,108 voyages and 9,202,995 disembarked in the summary — roughly 29 % of voyages and 31 % of arrivals. Any region-level figure drawn from it, including the mainland North America total, is a subset and not a national total.
  • The 1989 SCF Black median is suspect. The 6.0 % ratio for 1989 rests on a small Black subsample in that wave. It is flagged v2-verify, excluded from synthesis findings, and plotted but caveated on the wealth-gap page.

Educational disclaimer

DuBois is a research and educational dataset; figures are reconstructed from public sources for analysis and are not official government statistics. For authoritative figures, defer to the named original source for each dataset.
